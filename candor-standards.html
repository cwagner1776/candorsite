Mission

Why we exist, and what we'll never compromise on.

Candor exists because most people have no time to read five different newspapers to figure out what's actually happening. We use AI to do that reading at scale — synthesizing reporting from across the political spectrum into a single, framing-stripped account of the day's events.

That mission only works if you can trust what you read. This page is our contract with you: the standards we hold ourselves to, the rules our AI must follow, and the human review chain that catches what the AI misses.

If we ever fail one of these standards, we want you to be able to point at the specific clause we broke. That's why we wrote them down.

Core Principles

The Candor Pledge

Six commitments that govern every editorial decision we make.

We will not invent facts.

Every factual claim in a Candor article is traceable to a named source. Our AI is explicitly prohibited from inferring details that weren't in the source material, however plausible the inference.

We will not hide our sources.

Every sentence is attributed. Every source is listed publicly. Every source's known bias is disclosed. If we can't tell you where a claim came from, we don't publish it.

We will not replicate framing.

Adjectives, loaded verbs, and editorial characterizations from source outlets — including centrist ones — are stripped before publication. We aim for the underlying facts, not anyone's tone.

We will correct in public.

No silent edits. Every change of substance is logged on our Corrections page with the original and corrected text shown side by side.

We will not accept payment to alter coverage.

Advertisers, investors, and partners have zero influence on editorial decisions. The wall between business and editorial is enforced at the org-chart level — see Ownership & Funding.

We will tell you when we're uncertain.

Every article carries a confidence rating. Disputed claims are labeled, not smoothed over. If a story is still developing, we say so — clearly, at the top.

Independence rule

Two outlets running the same wire copy count as one source, not two. Our pipeline detects shared wire feeds (AP, Reuters, AFP) and weights them accordingly. The independence requirement exists because narrative consensus is not the same as factual consensus — five outlets repeating each other is not five sources.

Standards

Language & Framing Rules

The hardest part of stripping framing is knowing what to strip. Our style guide includes explicit rules about which classes of language must be removed, replaced, or attributed.

Use
  • Specific verbs that describe action (voted, signed, ruled, declined)
  • Documented quantities (56–44 vote, not "narrow margin")
  • Direct quotes with full attribution and context
  • Named actors (the senator, not "critics")
  • Both sides of a disputed claim, with labels
  • Plain language — short sentences, common words
Avoid
  • Loaded verbs (slammed, blasted, gushed, admitted)
  • Vague intensifiers (massive, sweeping, controversial)
  • Unsourced characterizations (some say, experts believe)
  • Headline framing that overstates ("crisis," "war on")
  • Smuggling editorial conclusions into "neutral" phrasing
  • Borrowing tone from any single source — even centrist ones

Our full language guide is internal and lists over 400 flagged terms. When the AI generates a sentence containing a flagged term, the term must be either removed, replaced with a documented alternative, or — if essential — placed inside an attributed quote.

Standards

Rules Our AI Must Follow

Candor's synthesis pipeline runs against a hard-coded set of constraints. These are not suggestions to the model — they are filters that block publication if violated.

No claims without source attribution.

Every sentence must have at least one source ID attached. Sentences that fail this check are removed before the article reaches the review queue.

No inference beyond the source material.

If three outlets say "the vote passed," the AI may not write "the vote passed easily" — even if that's likely true. Plausibility is not evidence.

No emotional language about identifiable people.

Descriptions of public figures must be limited to verifiable role, action, and direct quotes. The AI may not characterize a person's tone, mood, or apparent intent unless a source explicitly does so — in which case it must be attributed.

No fabricated quotes.

A quote in a Candor article must appear verbatim in at least one source. The AI is not permitted to paraphrase a statement as a direct quote, ever. Paraphrases are clearly labeled and not enclosed in quotation marks.

No predictions presented as facts.

Forecasts, projections, and analyst expectations must be attributed and tense-marked. "Markets are expected to fall" is not acceptable; "JPMorgan analysts forecast a 3% decline" is.

Mandatory human review tags.

Stories touching elections, court rulings, health, mass casualties, or named private individuals are auto-routed to human editors before publication, regardless of confidence score.

Standards

Anonymous Sources

Because Candor synthesizes rather than originates reporting, we generally do not directly use anonymous sources. We do, however, inherit them from our source outlets — and we have rules about how those inherited anonymous sources are handled.

A claim sourced only to anonymous individuals (e.g., "three administration officials," "a senior aide familiar with the matter") is treated as single-source regardless of how many outlets repeat it, unless the outlets demonstrably spoke to different unnamed individuals. We do not multiply anonymous attributions across outlets to manufacture consensus.

Anonymous-source claims appear in Candor articles with an explicit "Reported anonymously by [outlet]" label and are excluded from our high-confidence consensus tier. Readers can opt to filter them out entirely in their reading preferences.

When we'd break this rule

If a story of major public interest depends entirely on anonymous sources from a single outlet, we may publish a synthesis only with: (a) sign-off from our Editor-in-Chief, (b) an explicit notice at the top of the article, and (c) a confidence rating capped at Medium. This has happened twice in 2026.

Disclosure

Conflicts of Interest

Editorial staff are required to disclose financial holdings, political donations, family ties to elected officials or public-company executives, and any other relationship that could reasonably affect coverage. Disclosures are reviewed annually by our Standards Editor.

When a staff member has a relevant conflict on a specific story, they are recused from review of that story. Recusals are logged internally and disclosed to readers in the article's source notes when material.

Candor's parent company holds no equity stakes in news outlets, political organizations, or campaign-finance entities. The current ownership and funding structure is detailed below.

Disclosure

Ownership & Funding

Candor is an independent news product founded in 2026. It is not owned by, controlled by, or affiliated with any larger media group, political organization, or technology platform.

Funding. Candor is currently self-funded by its founder. It is designed to be sustained by readers through a single membership rather than by advertisers or outside investors. We do not run sponsored content, native ads, or affiliate links inside news articles.

No advertiser or political money. Candor takes no money from advertisers, and none — ever — from political donors or PACs. If the funding mix ever changes in a way that could affect editorial independence, that change will be disclosed plainly and prominently.

Editorial accountability. Final accountability for everything Candor publishes rests with the Editor-in-Chief. Commercial considerations never have read, edit, or pre-publication access to the editorial synthesis.

If this ever changes

If Candor is acquired, takes on a strategic investor with media holdings, or changes its revenue model in a way that affects editorial independence, we will disclose it at the top of every page for 30 days and explain what — if anything — changes about our standards.

People

Who's accountable

Candor is an AI-first product, but the responsibility for what it publishes is human and named. Editorial accountability — the standards on this page, the corrections process, and final sign-off on anything the sensitivity check flags — rests with the Editor-in-Chief.
