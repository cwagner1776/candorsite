Overview

The goal: the facts, without the frame.

Every story published on Candor begins as raw reporting from multiple news outlets — left-leaning, centrist, and right-leaning. Our AI reads all of them, identifies the factual claims they agree on, and writes a single synthesized article that presents those facts without the editorial framing any individual outlet applies.

We don't hide where we got the information. Every sentence is traceable to a source. Every source is labeled with its known bias rating. You can always see the original framing by switching to the Compare Coverage view.

Step 1

Source Ingestion

Candor continuously monitors over 50,000 news outlets via RSS feeds, licensed news APIs, and direct editorial partnerships. When a new story is detected across multiple outlets — meaning at least two independent sources are covering the same event — it enters the rewriting pipeline.

We do not scrape full article text without authorization. We use licensed data feeds, headline and summary data covered under fair use, and editorial partnerships with select publishers. Our Source List details every outlet and the type of access we have.

Coverage threshold

A story must be covered by a minimum of 2 independent outlets before Candor will publish a synthesis. Stories covered by only one source are flagged as "Developing" and held until corroborated.

Step 2

AI Rewriting Process

Once a story has sufficient coverage, Candor's AI processes all available sources simultaneously. The rewriting pipeline follows a strict sequence:

1
Claim extraction

The AI identifies discrete factual claims from each source — who, what, when, where — and maps them to a common event structure, separating facts from editorial commentary.

2
Consensus scoring

Claims are cross-referenced across sources. Claims verified by 3 or more sources receive a High confidence rating. Claims from only one source are included only if that source has a strong verification track record, and are labeled accordingly.

3
Framing removal

Editorial language — adjectives, loaded verbs, characterizations, and tone markers — is stripped from the synthesized article. The AI is explicitly instructed not to replicate the framing of any source, even centrist ones.

4
Attribution tagging

Every sentence in the final article is tagged with its source(s). These tags are visible in the article view and expandable so readers can verify the original reporting directly.

5
Human review queue

High-sensitivity stories — breaking news, political events, and stories with contested facts — are flagged for human editorial review before publication. Our editorial team can hold, correct, or annotate any AI-generated article.

Step 3

Bias Classification

Every source in the Candor network is assigned a bias rating on a five-point scale. Ratings are based on the AllSides Media Bias Ratings, cross-referenced with Ad Fontes Media's Media Bias Chart and our own editorial analysis. Ratings are reviewed annually and updated when significant editorial changes occur at an outlet.

Rating Description Example Outlets
Left Consistent liberal or progressive editorial framing; language and story selection skew toward left-of-center perspectives. Guardian, MSNBC, Vox, Mother Jones
Lean Left Mild liberal framing; generally factual with some editorial tilt in story selection and language. New York Times, Washington Post, NPR
Center Minimal detectable bias; balanced story selection and neutral language across political topics. Reuters, AP, BBC, Bloomberg
Lean Right Mild conservative framing; generally factual with some editorial tilt toward right-of-center perspectives. Wall Street Journal, The Hill, Newsweek
Right Consistent conservative editorial framing; language and story selection skew toward right-of-center perspectives. Fox News, NY Post, Washington Examiner
Important note

Bias ratings describe editorial framing tendencies, not factual reliability. A left-leaning outlet can publish highly accurate reporting. A centrist outlet can make factual errors. Candor's consensus scoring is independent of bias classification.

Step 4

Fact Verification

Candor does not independently verify facts in the field — we are a synthesis platform, not a primary reporting organization. Our verification process is based on cross-source consensus: if multiple independent outlets with different editorial biases agree on a factual claim, we treat it as verified for publication purposes.

For claims that are contested between sources, Candor presents the disagreement explicitly rather than choosing a side. These are labeled "Disputed" in the article view. For claims sourced from only one outlet, we label them with that outlet's name and bias rating so readers can apply their own judgment.

We partner with third-party fact-checkers including PolitiFact and Snopes for verification of specific political claims. When a fact-check exists for a claim in a Candor article, a link to that check is displayed inline.

Step 5

Confidence Scoring

Every Candor article displays a Factual Consensus score and a Confidence rating. These are calculated as follows:

Factual Consensus % — the percentage of discrete factual claims in the article that are verified by 3 or more independent sources. A score of 94% means 94% of claims in that article appear across at least 3 sources.

Confidence rating — a qualitative label derived from the consensus score, number of sources, and recency of coverage:

Limitations

What Candor can't do

We believe in being honest about the limits of AI-assisted journalism.

We can't detect coordinated narratives. If multiple outlets across the political spectrum are all repeating the same false claim — for example, because it originated from a government press release — Candor's consensus scoring will treat it as verified. We are not immune to coordinated misinformation at scale.

We can't replace primary reporting. Candor does not have reporters in the field. Everything we publish is derived from the work of journalists at other outlets. If a story isn't being covered by our source network, Candor won't have it.

Our bias ratings are imperfect. Media bias is contested terrain, and reasonable people disagree about where any given outlet sits. We use the best available third-party data and review our ratings regularly, but they are not infallible.

AI makes mistakes. Our rewriting pipeline is reviewed by human editors for high-sensitivity stories, but not every article receives human review before publication. If you spot an error, please use our Corrections process.

Update Frequency

How often stories are updated

Breaking stories are updated continuously as new sources are ingested — typically every 3–15 minutes for major events. Each update re-runs the full synthesis pipeline against the expanded source set, which may change the consensus score, confidence rating, and article text.

Stories more than 48 hours old are moved to archive status and no longer receive automatic updates. Archived stories can be manually updated by our editorial team if significant new information emerges.

The timestamp displayed on every Candor article reflects the last time the synthesis was run — not the time the original story broke. A story published yesterday morning but updated 12 minutes ago reflects our most current synthesis of all available reporting.
