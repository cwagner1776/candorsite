Mistakes are inevitable. Hiding them isn't.

Candor is an AI-assisted news platform, and AI makes mistakes. So do humans. So do the source outlets we synthesize from. When an error reaches publication — whether ours or one we inherited — we correct it openly, log it on this page, and explain what went wrong.

Every correction below shows the exact text we changed, the date and time the change was made, and a link to the article in its corrected form. Articles with active corrections display a notice at the top.

If you've spotted something we got wrong, use the form on this page. We respond to every submission, usually within 24 hours.

How corrections work at Candor

Our four-tier system
01
Correction

A factual error in the published article — a wrong name, number, date, attribution, or location. The article remains live with the corrected text. A notice appears at the top showing what changed.

02
Clarification

The original statement was technically accurate but framed in a way that could mislead — a missing qualifier, an ambiguous phrase, or context that materially changes the reader's understanding.

03
Update

New information has emerged that materially changes the story but does not invalidate the original reporting. The article is updated with a timestamp and a summary of what's new.

04
Retraction

The article's core premise is unsupportable. We remove the original article, replace it with a retraction notice explaining why, and keep that notice live indefinitely. Retractions are rare and we log every one.

05
Source retraction

When one of our upstream source outlets retracts a story we synthesized from, we re-run the synthesis against the remaining sources. If the story can't stand without the retracted source, we issue our own retraction.

06
No silent edits

We never edit a published article to fix an error without a public correction notice. The only silent changes we make are typo fixes that don't affect meaning. Every other change is logged here.

Spotted an error?

Send us the correction. We'll respond within 24 hours.

Every submission is reviewed by our editorial team. If you're the subject of an article, please use the dedicated subject-of-article form instead — it routes to a senior editor with shorter response targets.

Frequently asked

About our process
What's the difference between a correction and a clarification?
A correction fixes something that was factually wrong — a misspelled name, a wrong number, a misattributed quote. A clarification addresses something that was technically accurate but written in a way that misled readers. Both are logged here with equal visibility.
Do you ever delete articles?
Only in the case of a full retraction, where the article's core premise is unsupportable. Even then, the URL remains live and is replaced with the retraction notice — so anyone who linked to the original can see what happened. We never delete an article to hide a mistake.
What if the error came from one of your source outlets?
We still correct it on our end and log it here. Our synthesis is our responsibility — if we passed along an error from a source, that's our error too. Where relevant, we note the source-chain in the correction. See our Methodology for how source disagreements are handled before publication.
How quickly do you respond to reader submissions?
Median response time in 2026 is under 19 hours from submission to either a published correction or a written explanation of why we believe the article is accurate. Submissions concerning breaking-news stories are prioritized.
Who reviews corrections?
Our editorial team — two senior editors and three associate editors — reviews every submission. Significant corrections (retractions, attribution errors involving public figures, and any correction caught by the subject of an article) require sign-off from our Editor-in-Chief. See Editorial Standards for the full review chain.
