How Candor started.

The idea for Candor started with a simple, frustrating observation: you can read the same news event across a left-leaning daily, a centrist wire, and a right-leaning outlet, and come away with three completely different impressions of what happened. The facts are usually nearly identical. The headlines, the framing, and the emotional register are not.

One reader comes away convinced the country is sliding into chaos. Another comes away convinced it's finally being set right. They read the same news. They just didn't read the same story.

"The factual common ground is almost always there. It just gets buried under choreography — and the choreography is what people end up fighting about."

— Carson Wagner, Founder

I saw a gap. The work of reading widely, comparing how outlets frame the same event, and pulling out the facts they actually agree on is exactly the kind of work modern AI is good at — and almost nobody has the time to do it for themselves. Used carefully, AI could deliver that to far more people, on far better terms than the attention-driven model the rest of the internet runs on.

So that's what Candor is built to do, automatically, for every story, every day. The AI reads dozens of outlets across the political spectrum, isolates the factual claims they agree on, strips the editorial framing, and produces a single synthesis with every claim attributed back to its original source.

What you get is shorter, plainer, and — the goal is — closer to what actually happened. What you also get is a button on every story labeled Compare Coverage. Press it and you can see exactly how each outlet framed the same event. Candor doesn't hide the partisans. It just doesn't let them write the synthesis.

Candor is independent and self-funded, and it's built around a hard rule: every claim is traceable, and the AI never operates without human editorial standards governing what it is and isn't allowed to do. It's early, and it's honest about being early — including being public, every time, when it gets something wrong.

What We Believe

Three things we'll never trade away.

Principle One

Show the work.

Every sentence we publish is traceable to a named source. Every source is labeled with its known bias. Every framing decision is reversible by the reader. We'd rather show our work and be wrong than hide it and be plausibly right.

Principle Two

Sell to readers, not to anyone else.

Our revenue comes from people who pay to read us. Not from advertisers who pay to be next to us, not from investors who want us to grow at any cost, not from foundations with policy agendas. The incentive has to point at the reader or it eventually points away from them.

Principle Three

Be honest about the AI.

Candor's pipeline is AI-first. That's not something to hide and it's not something to celebrate — it's just how it's built. The aim is to be explicit about what the model does well, where it fails, and which decisions stay under human editorial control.

Why we publish this.

It's impossible to evaluate a news outlet without knowing who pays for it. Candor is early and independent: today it's self-funded by its founder, and it's designed to be sustained by readers through a single membership — not by advertisers, investors with editorial input, or political money.

The business model is deliberately simple so the incentive stays pointed at the reader. If that funding mix ever changes in a way that could affect editorial independence, it will be disclosed plainly and prominently.
